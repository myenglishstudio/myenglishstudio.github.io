1 Not rocket science
If something “isn't rocket science”, it isn't difficult to do or to understand: “Using this new accounting system isn't exactly rocket science – you just need to read over the 2-page manual and learn a few of the tricks.”

2 To tighten your belt
If a company has to “tighten its belt”, it has to cut costs and spend less money: “With the economy doing so badly, many companies are tightening their belts.”

3 Not set in stone
If something “isn't set in stone”, it isn't fixed or permanent, so it won't be difficult to change: “The meeting time isn't set in stone, so feel free to suggest an alternative day or time.”
4
Shape up or ship out
Click to flip
4 Shape up or ship out!
If you tell someone to “shape up or ship out”, you're telling them to either improve (shape up) or leave (ship out): “I've had enough of your negative attitude – either shape up or ship out!”
5

5 Pull the plug
If you “pull the plug” on a project (for example), you stop it, often because it isn't going well: “After wasting millions investing on the new product and with very few sales, they finally decided to pull the plug on it.”
6

6 Give someone a heads up
If you “give someone a heads up”, you warn them about something bad, dangerous or worrying that might happen in the future: “This is just a heads up to let you know that the new president is coming next week to inspect the office.”
7
Give you the hard sell
Click to flip
7 Give you the hard sell
If someone “gives you the hard sell”, they are very persuasive, insistent and aggressive as they try to sell you something: “I hate it when I'm in a store and are the shop assistant gives me the hard sell.”
8
A back-of-the-envelope (calculation)
Click to flip
8 A back-of-the-envelope
A “back-of-the-envelope calculation” is a quick calculation on a piece of paper (such as an envelope) that will give you a general understanding of the costs, figures or numbers: “I don't need the exact numbers right now – just give me a back-of-the-envelope calculation.”
9

9 Throw good money after bad
If you “throw good money after bad”, you waste money by spending it on something that's useless, worthless, or impossible to repair: “Trying to fix this old car is like throwing good money after bad. It would be cheaper to get a new one.”
10

10 Reality check
If someone needs a “reality check”, they need to know the truth about a situation because they don't understand what's really happening: “If you think your company can compete against all the other big ones, you need a reality check.”
11

11 Jump through hoops
If you “jump through hoops”, you do what people want so you can get something. These things often appear to be ridiculous or unnecessary, but you have to do them if you want that thing: “We had to jump through hoops to get the bank loan but it was worth it in the end.”
12

12 Keep your eye on the prize
If you “keep your eye on the prize”, you focus on the end result, and you try not to let the small problems distract you: “I know there's a lot of work to do right now, but just keep your eye on the prize: this time next year we'll be making a lot of money!”
13

13 Keep something under wraps
If you “keep an idea or plan under wraps”, you don't tell anyone about it and it is a secret: “I can't tell you anything about the new product right now – they told us to keep it all under wraps.”
14

14 My gut tells me
If your “gut tells you something”, you know what to do naturally, without having to think about it or analyse it: “I don't know Jenny very well, but my gut tells me that she'd be perfect for the job.”
15

15 Pass the buck
If someone “passes the buck”, they blame somebody else for their mistakes: “Losing that contract was your fault, so stop trying to pass the buck!”
